Fraud Types Detected

Based on IMF Working Paper WP/07/31 and IMF How-To Note 2023/001

🕳️

Missing Trader (MTIC)

Trader collects VAT on sales then disappears without remitting to the authority.

  • Inactive traders after heavy imports
  • Buyer input-VAT claims with no seller declaration
  • Consecutive late/missed filings
  • Never-filed after registration
IMF WP/07/31 §3.1
🎠

Carousel Fraud

Goods traded in a circular chain across entities to generate false VAT refund claims.

  • Graph cycle detection (NetworkX)
  • High-risk commodities (phones, CPUs, gold)
  • Rapid resale ≤ 24 hours
  • Cross-border chain involvement
IMF WP/07/31 §3.2
🔄

Contra-Trading

Two parallel chains — one legitimate, one fraudulent — whose VAT liabilities cancel out.

  • Near-zero net VAT on high gross turnover
  • Trading in 3+ unrelated sectors simultaneously
  • Mirrored buy/sell chain timing
IMF WP/07/31 §3.3
🏭

Invoice Mill / Bogus Trader

Shell company created solely to generate fraudulent invoices for VAT credit claims.

  • High daily invoice volume (≥ 30/day)
  • Suspiciously round invoice amounts
  • No purchase invoices of their own
  • Benford's Law chi-squared anomaly
IMF WP/07/31 §3.4
💸

VAT Refund Fraud

False or inflated VAT refund claims, often exploiting export zero-rating.

  • Refund > 80% of output VAT
  • Sudden refund spike vs. history
  • Frequent return amendments
  • Refund from flagged suppliers
IMF HTN 2023/001
👻

Phantom Exporter

Claims export zero-rating for goods that were never physically exported.

  • Export invoices without customs declarations
  • > 50% of exports lack customs docs
  • Cross-checked via Customs System API
IMF HTN 2023/001 §4.2
📊

Benford's Law Anomaly

Invoice amounts that don't follow the expected leading-digit frequency distribution — a signature of fabricated figures.

  • Chi-squared goodness-of-fit test
  • Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD) scoring
  • Weekly automated scan
Statistical detection
🕸️

Network Anomaly

Suspicious transaction network structures revealing hidden relationships between traders.

  • Graph betweenness centrality (buffer traders)
  • Strongly connected components
  • Cross-border cluster detection
Graph analytics
